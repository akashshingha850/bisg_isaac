# Anchor heading to walls before closing loops

On this stack the fastest, cheapest way to bound drift is not a loop-closure system. It is an **absolute, drift-free yaw measurement taken from the room's own walls (a "Manhattan compass")**, fed into EKF2 through a continuous, rate-limited correction layer between the ZED and MAVROS. The dominant error here is heading slip during in-place turns (2-8° per 90° turn). Loop closure, relocalization and depth-to-map registration all remove that error only when the drone comes back to a place it has already seen. A drone exploring unknown rooms mostly does not. Structural-regularity papers show that an absolute heading prior is the real lever. StructVIO cut indoor drift from about **1.08% to 0.29% of distance**, and generic line features without the heading prior gave no gain at all ([StructVIO, T-RO 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06796)). Loop-closure systems are mature, but their published value is about a **30-60% APE reduction on trajectories that revisit** ([cuVSLAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04359)). On this stack they also bring a specific hazard. PX4 EKF2 v1.17 handles a vision jump as either an ordinary innovation, which becomes a velocity transient, or a hard "teleport" reset. Stock MAVROS cannot send the `reset_counter` that would announce the jump, and the ZED wrapper's default `reset_odom_with_loop_closure: true` makes the `odom` topic itself snap back to the origin on every loop closure. The recommended order is: (1) make the PX4-facing odometry provably continuous; (2) fix the EKF2 v1.17 yaw-noise quirk; (3) add a gravity-levelled Manhattan yaw compass with ramped corrections; (4) add wall-plane offsets for position; and only then (5) use ZED GEN_3 or cuVSLAM loop closure and prior-map relocalization, in a `map→odom` layer the planner consumes, never as raw EV jumps.

## Heading slip, not missing loop closure, sets the error budget

A yaw error ε turns every metre flown into roughly ε·1 m of cross-track error: about 1.7 cm per degree per metre. One 90° turn that slips 2-8° adds 3.5-14 cm of lateral error per metre flown afterwards. Because heading error never decays without an absolute reference, a few turns stack into the 10-12° range that gives the observed ~0.2 m/m. The magnetometer is off, and EKF2 with mag type None takes heading only from GPS or external vision ([PX4 EKF guide](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/advanced_config/tuning_the_ecl_ekf)). EKF2's heading is therefore exactly the ZED's heading, slip included. Nothing in the autopilot can catch it.

The literature agrees that rotation, not translation, is the thing to fix. OPVO's motivating experiment found that giving a feature VO the ground-truth rotation makes its position drift largely disappear, and concluded that "drift of the rotation estimate is the main source of position inaccuracy in VO" ([OPVO, BMVC 2017](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2017visual.pdf)). A recent GTSAM VIO study for indoor UAVs reports accumulated yaw drift as the dominant residual error on non-revisiting EuRoC trajectories ([MDPI Drones](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/10/9/685)). VINS-Mono builds its loop-closure pose graph in exactly **4 DoF (x, y, z, yaw)**, because roll and pitch are already observable from gravity ([VINS-Mono, T-RO 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03852)). Yaw is the one rotational degree of freedom an IMU cannot anchor.

This is why loop closure is the wrong first tool for an exploration drone. It needs a revisit. Scan-to-map depth registration needs one too: while exploring, every surface in view was inserted into the map from the already-drifted pose, so registration only removes jitter. KISS-ICP, a strong scan-to-map odometry, still drifts about **0.5% of distance travelled** on KITTI routes that rarely revisit ([KISS-ICP, RA-L 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.15397)). A Manhattan constraint needs only that a wall be in view. One plausible exception deserves a test. An in-place 360° spin returns the camera to its starting view, which is a self-revisit that GEN_3 loop closure could catch. A 90° turn never does.

## A gravity-levelled Manhattan compass bounds yaw from a single wall

Box rooms and warehouses are close to ideal Manhattan worlds: floor, walls and rack faces sit on three orthogonal directions. Real-sensor and VIO results, not just synthetic ones, show absolute yaw from that structure in the low single degrees, with no accumulation:

| Method (year, venue) | Mechanism | Key numbers | Fit for ZED + PX4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| RTMF, Straub et al. (2015, IROS) | Manhattan frame from depth normals, fused with gyro in a quaternion EKF | 19.8 ms/frame on a laptop GPU at 640×480. Against Vicon over 4.5 min: <2.8° RMSE fused, <3.4° standalone with zero drift, vs 8.50° and drifting for IMU only ([PDF](https://jstraub.github.io/download/straub2015rtmf_web.pdf)) | Direct template for a yaw-only compass |
| OPVO, Kim et al. (2017, BMVC) | MF rotation by SO(3) mean shift, then de-rotated translation | Needs two orthogonal planes in view at all times; fails on a single plane ([LPVO PDF](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2018low.pdf)) | Superseded once gravity is used |
| LPVO, Kim et al. (2018, ICRA) | Adds vanishing directions from lines; one plane plus one line pair suffices | ICL-NUIM rotation error 0.22° vs ORB 1.63°. Corridor loop drift <0.3% over 88 m with four pure rotations ([PDF](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2018low.pdf)) | Evidence that pure rotations are survivable |
| L-SLAM, Kim et al. (2018, ECCV) | With rotation fixed, planes become scalar offsets in a linear KF | ICL-NUIM ATE 0.038 m; 120 m corridor final drift <0.1% with no loop closure; >20 Hz on an i5 ([PDF](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2018linear.pdf)) | Template for wall-relative position |
| ManhattanSLAM, Yunus et al. (2021, ICRA) | ORB-SLAM2 + MF rotation when detected, feature fallback otherwise | ICL-NUIM 0.014 m. TAMU corridor loop drift 0.53 m with MF vs 0.77 m without. An MF was found in only 0-26 of 600-3,669 frames on cluttered TUM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15068)) | Shows the detector goes silent in clutter |
| StructVIO, Zou et al. (2019, T-RO) | MSCKF + structural lines; per-local-MW heading kept in the state (Atlanta world) | 16 runs of 190-650 m: mean drift 0.292% vs OKVIS 1.078% and VINS (no loop) 1.410%. Forcing a single Manhattan world on oblique corridors raised RMSE ~287% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06796)) | Strongest VIO evidence; heading prior is the gain |
| PGD-VIO, Zhang et al. (2024, arXiv) | RGB-D-inertial EKF with plane states plus duplicate-wall drift detection | CID-SIMS Floor14_2: 1.750 m (OpenVINS) → 1.704 m (ov_plane) → 0.163 m. Plane states alone cut Office 1 from 0.097 to 0.030 m ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17709)) | Shows depth-backed planes beat monocular planes |
| Doer & Trommer (2021, ICINS) | Radar-inertial EKF with Manhattan yaw aiding from a single scan | Flight-tested indoors on drones; accuracy similar to VIO, many times faster than real time on embedded hardware ([author page](https://christopherdoer.github.io/publications/2021_05_ICINS2021)) | Closest flight-proven analogue of a loosely coupled compass |

Gravity is the key design choice. OPVO needs two visible planes because it estimates full 3-D rotation from structure alone. On a drone, the VIO/IMU roll and pitch are already drift-free, so the compass only has to recover yaw, and **one vertical wall is enough**. The recipe, mostly from Straub and OPVO, runs as follows. Compute normals from ZED depth at about 320×180, using guided-filter smoothing then a Sobel cross product, or integral-image tangent averaging ([Straub](https://jstraub.github.io/download/straub2015rtmf_web.pdf); [OPVO](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2017visual.pdf)). Rotate the normals into a gravity-levelled frame using VIO roll and pitch, and keep only near-horizontal ones. Estimate the room axis as atan2(Σ sin 4θ, Σ cos 4θ)/4, a fourfold-folded azimuth histogram. Unwrap modulo 90° around the current VIO yaw, then accept the measurement only if the innovation is under 10-20°, enough normals support it, and the peak is sharp. The 90° ambiguity cannot slip, because consistent tracking only needs inter-update rotation under 45° ([Straub](https://jstraub.github.io/download/straub2015rtmf_web.pdf)). With updates at 1 Hz or faster, even an 8° per-turn slip stays far inside that margin. The room frame is fixed once at takeoff as the MF axis nearest the initial VIO yaw.

The failure modes are well documented and can be gated. Straub's estimate degraded when normals fell on only one or two axes, and locked onto a local MW in a hallway misaligned with the building ([Straub](https://jstraub.github.io/download/straub2015rtmf_web.pdf)). ManhattanSLAM's detector rarely fired in cluttered real rooms, and "MW-based methods suffer" on noisy real depth compared with synthetic ICL-NUIM ([ManhattanSLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15068)). StructVIO showed that oblique wings break a single-MW assumption, which argues for an Atlanta mode (gravity plus several horizontal headings, each a state) in irregular warehouses ([StructVIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06796)). Two risks are specific to this stack and not covered in any paper. First, no source reports Manhattan yaw accuracy from stereo depth rather than structured light. Second, the ZED's fill modes can paint smooth fake surfaces on textureless walls, which look strongly planar while being wrong ([Stereolabs depth docs](https://www.stereolabs.com/docs/depth-sensing)). Mask by depth confidence and turn fill off for the compass input. The 2-3° figure is therefore a target to measure in the Isaac Sim Warehouse, not a guarantee. Even so, a bounded 3° error caps cross-track error near 5 cm/m for the whole flight, where today's error compounds turn after turn.

Wall-relative position follows directly. Once yaw is fixed to the room axes, L-SLAM shows that each wall becomes a single scalar offset and the camera-to-wall distance becomes a linear measurement ([L-SLAM](http://brian.coltin.org/pub/kim2018linear.pdf)). The floor bounds height, and two orthogonal walls bound x and y. A corridor or warehouse aisle, where only parallel walls are visible, leaves position along the aisle unconstrained. That direction must still come from VIO, or from an aisle-end or cross-rack wall when one is in view. Stereo depth noise grows with z². Stereolabs quotes about 1% of distance near and up to about 9% at maximum range ([Stereolabs depth docs](https://www.stereolabs.com/docs/depth-sensing)). Wall offsets should therefore come from surfaces within roughly 4-5 m and be weighted by 1/σ². The ZED Mini's narrower baseline makes this worse than the 120 mm-baseline figures in the notes.

## EKF2 v1.17 teleports on resets, and MAVROS cannot announce them

How a correction enters EKF2 matters as much as where it comes from. Reading the v1.17 source shows that indoors, with no GNSS, **EKF2 has no gradual-absorption path for EV position offsets**. The EV position bias estimator, the one mechanism that would soak up an offset slowly, is activated only when GNSS position fusion is active ([ev_pos_control.cpp L73-L78](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_pos_control.cpp#L61-L181)). Every vision step therefore ends up in one of three places:

| Step size relative to gate | What EKF2 v1.17 does | Effect on the vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Below ~5σ per axis (`EKF2_EVP_GATE` = 5, floor 0.1 m noise) | Fused as an ordinary measurement through the Kalman gain | Residual leaks into velocity and accel-bias states, giving a velocity transient the position loop reacts to |
| Above the gate, no `reset_counter` change | Rejected for 1 s (hard-coded `no_aid_timeout_max`), then hard reset to EV ("EV position fusion failing, resetting") | A teleport, plus a lost second of aiding |
| After 5 such in-air resets | EV position fusion stops | Vehicle loses position aiding |

Sources: [ev_pos_control.cpp L269-L307](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_pos_control.cpp#L269-L307); [common.h L480-L482](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/common.h#L480-L482); [ekf.h](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/ekf.h).

The gate is per axis, `innov² / (gate² · innov_var)`, not a joint Mahalanobis test. A `reset_counter` change skips the 1 s wait and resets immediately. On that reset EKF2 re-seeds the EV-to-EKF attitude filter, resets yaw to EV yaw when EV yaw is fused, and resets velocity only if EV velocity is the sole horizontal aid ([ev_control.cpp](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_control.cpp#L52); [ev_yaw_control.cpp](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_yaw_control.cpp#L64-L138); [ev_vel_control.cpp](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_vel_control.cpp#L64-L116)). The counter makes a jump clean. It does not make it smaller.

Three facts about the current pipeline follow, and each is actionable. First, the **MAVROS ROS 2 `odom` plugin value-initialises the ODOMETRY message and never assigns `reset_counter` or `quality`**, so both are always 0. `vision_pose` sets neither ([mavros odom.cpp](https://github.com/mavlink/mavros/blob/ros2/mavros_extras/src/plugins/odom.cpp#L258-L360); [vision_pose_estimate.cpp](https://github.com/mavlink/mavros/blob/ros2/mavros_extras/src/plugins/vision_pose_estimate.cpp#L121-L162)). The bench's reset-counter tally in `tests/vio_eval.py` will therefore always read zero. `EKF2_EV_QMIN` cannot be used as a degradation switch either: with QMIN above 0, a constant quality of 0 would block EV fusion entirely ([ev_control.cpp L55](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_control.cpp#L55-L63)).

Second, the ZED wrapper defaults to `reset_odom_with_loop_closure: true`. On `LOOP_CLOSED` its code sets odom→base to identity and publishes a zero twist for that frame ([zed_camera_component_main.cpp L6754-L6907](https://github.com/stereolabs/zed-ros2-wrapper/blob/master/zed_components/src/zed_camera/src/zed_camera_component_main.cpp#L6745-L6907); [common_stereo.yaml](https://github.com/stereolabs/zed-ros2-wrapper/blob/master/zed_wrapper/config/common_stereo.yaml#L117-L141)). The bridge forwards `odom`, so if area memory is on with this default, a loop closure produces the worst case: a jump to the origin, rejected for 1 s, then a hard reset that burns one of the five. That breaks REP 105's requirement that `odom` evolve "without discrete jumps" ([REP 105](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0105.html)). The bridge's `odom` is "never loop-corrected" only as long as this flag is false or area memory is off. Check the launch config before trusting it.

Third, a v1.17 bug inflates the yaw noise. `ev_yaw_control.cpp` floors the yaw variance with `EKF2_EVA_NOISE` **unsquared**, so the default 0.1 rad gives a variance of 0.1 rad² (σ ≈ 18°) instead of 0.01 rad² (σ ≈ 5.7°). `main` fixes this by squaring the parameter ([1.17 L48](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_yaw_control.cpp#L48); [main L48](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/main/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/aid_sources/external_vision/ev_yaw_control.cpp#L48)). With the 2.6σ heading gate, the effective yaw gate is then at least about 47°, so EKF2 will fuse almost any yaw step. On v1.17, set `EKF2_EVA_NOISE` to the desired variance rather than the standard deviation, for example about 0.0012 for σ ≈ 2°. The bench already records this quirk.

Given those mechanics, the way to feed corrections "without jumps" is to keep them out of the EKF's step detector entirely. Build a small **correction layer** between ZED `odom` and `/mavros/odometry/out`. This is an engineering inference from the code, not documented PX4 practice. The layer holds a correction yaw ψc and offset, and moves them toward their targets (the compass yaw, a wall offset, or a loop-closure `map→odom`) at a capped rate of a few degrees and a few cm per second. It then integrates odometry increments rather than absolute poses: p_out(t) = p_out(t−1) + Rz(ψc)·Δp_odom, and yaw_out = yaw_odom + ψc. Position stays continuous, each 30 Hz sample changes heading by hundredths of a degree, and body-frame twist is untouched. Each sample stays far inside the 5σ position and 2.6σ heading gates, so EKF2 absorbs the correction as ordinary measurements. Because slip happens during discrete in-place turns, the exploration planner can add a short hover after each turn so a 2-8° correction converges at 1-2°/s before forward flight resumes. The PX4 docs' remark that "it is more reliable to just use vision velocity" when vision position disagrees with another source supports the same idea ([PX4 VIO](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/computer_vision/visual_inertial_odometry)). The bench's `EKF2_EV_CTRL: 12` (velocity plus yaw) ablation is the right control experiment: it removes position jumps completely and leaves heading as the only coupling.

When a true discontinuity cannot be avoided, such as a relocalization after `LOST`, the counter must reach PX4. There are two ways. One is a few-line patch to MAVROS `odom.cpp` that sets `msg.reset_counter` and `msg.quality` from a side topic before sending. The other is to bypass MAVROS and publish `px4_msgs/VehicleOdometry` on `/fmu/in/vehicle_visual_odometry` via uXRCE-DDS, since that message carries both fields ([VehicleOdometry.msg](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/msg/versioned/VehicleOdometry.msg); [mavlink_receiver.cpp L1533-L1534](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/release/1.17/src/modules/mavlink/mavlink_receiver.cpp#L1350-L1548)). Either way, an offboard controller holding local-frame position setpoints will be displaced by each reset unless it applies the `delta_xy`/`delta_heading` that EKF2 publishes. PX4's maintainer says onboard modes compensate automatically, while offboard compensation is the companion's job ([PX4 #19830](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/19830)). A user who did this from the reset counter still saw a setpoint spike from round-trip delay ([same issue](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/19830)). Robust filtering does not remove the need for this separation. Chi-square gates, Student-t filters and switchable constraints all exist ([Sensors 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/7/2036); [Sünderhauf & Protzel, IROS 2012](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/109582)), but no gate can tell a legitimate loop-closure step from an outlier. Systems that work route corrections through a frame transform or an explicit reset flag. Use a companion-side Mahalanobis gate on VIO increments against the IMU prediction, and drive degradation handling from ZED's `pose_confidence` (GEN_3, SDK ≥ 5.4) and `spatial_memory_status` (`LOST` is documented as "signal to stop the robot") ([ZED ROS 2 msgs](https://docs.stereolabs.com/docs/integrations/ros-2/custom-msgs)).

## Loop closure and map registration bound drift only where the drone returns

Loop closure is still worth having for return-to-home, for multi-room missions and for building a prior map for repeat photography runs. The options in reach of this stack differ more in integration style than in accuracy:

| System (year, venue) | Loop closure / output style | Key numbers | Stack fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZED SDK GEN_3 (SDK 5.x, vendor) | Area memory, `.area` save/relocalize; correction in `map→odom` | Warehouse mean/max APE 0.56/1.2 m over ~400 m sequences on a ZED X; Orin NX 13% CPU / 44% GPU ([Stereolabs modes](https://docs.stereolabs.com/docs/development/zed-sdk/modules/positional-tracking/modes)) | Already in the stack; pin ≥ 5.5.1 |
| cuVSLAM / `isaac_ros_cuvslam` (2025, arXiv) | Async pose graph; separate `odom→base` and `map→base` | Stereo-inertial 3.8 ms/frame on AGX Orin. EuRoC SI APE 0.19 m odom → 0.13 m SLAM; TUM-VI room 0.18 → 0.12 m ([cuVSLAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04359)) | Native to the Isaac/ROS 2 world; needs ZED stereo + calibrated IMU extrinsic |
| ORB-SLAM3 (2021, T-RO) | DBoW2, Atlas map merging; one map-relative pose that jumps | EuRoC stereo-inertial 0.035 m; TUM-VI rooms 0.009 m; magistrale ~1 m over up to 900 m ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)) | Most accurate; community ROS 2 ports only |
| VINS-Fusion (2019 code; T-RO 2018) | Optional 4-DoF `loop_fusion` beside VIO | EuRoC SI 0.138 m (default config, run by the ORB-SLAM3 authors) ([ORB-SLAM3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)) | ROS 1 officially |
| Kimera (2020, ICRA) | DBoW2 + PCM-robust pose graph, separate from VIO | MH_4 0.24 → 0.15 m; but V1_2 0.08 → 0.11 m. Without PCM, V1_01 1.59 m vs 0.049 m ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02490)) | Shows false loops are catastrophic |

Three patterns emerge from these numbers. First, at room scale, loop closure barely improves local accuracy and can make it worse. cuVSLAM's 1 s RPE on TUM-VI rooms is unchanged (0.023 vs 0.025 m) ([cuVSLAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04359)), and Kimera got worse on small Vicon rooms ([Kimera](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02490)). Its value is bounding global error on revisits, not smoothing flight. Second, warehouses with repeated racking are where false loops live. Stereolabs fixed "false positive loop closure … in low-textured areas" only in SDK 5.3.0. In 5.5.1 it fixed GEN_3 "drifting away by up to several meters after tracking was briefly lost", and changed loop closure so it no longer bends already-correct trajectory sections ([zed-sdk releases](https://github.com/stereolabs/zed-sdk/releases); [5.5.1](https://github.com/stereolabs/zed-sdk/releases/tag/5.5.1)). Stereolabs' own mapping guidance is to keep single loops under about 20 m and to double the loops in repetitive spaces ([Area memory tutorial](https://docs.stereolabs.com/docs/development/zed-sdk/modules/positional-tracking/vslam-mapping-tutorial)). Third, revisits must face the same way. Many TUM-VI sequences produced no loop at all because start and end views were opposite ([ORB-SLAM3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)). The strongest drift bound in the literature is multi-session map reuse, which brought ORB-SLAM3's errors "to centimeter level" ([ORB-SLAM3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)). For repeat photography of a known warehouse, that argues for a survey flight that saves a `.area` (or cuVSLAM `SaveMap`) and later flights in localization mode.

The architecture that makes any of these safe is REP 105's: `odom` continuous, all jumps in `map→odom` ([REP 105](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0105.html)). cuVSLAM, VINS-Fusion, Kimera and the ZED wrapper already publish that split. The `map→odom` delta becomes a target for the ramped correction layer above, or simply transforms mission goals from map to odom before they go to PX4. Stereolabs does not document whether a GEN_3 correction is a step or a blend, or how large typical corrections are. Measure that in the sim before choosing ramp rates. Treat any `LOOP_CLOSED` whose implied correction exceeds a plausible drift bound (say a few times the expected drift since the last closure) as a suspect loop and do not apply it.

Depth-to-map registration against a TSDF or voxel map is the geometric cousin of loop closure, and the same revisit caveat applies. Its specific hazard is degeneracy. A forward ZED looking at one wall constrains only one translation and two rotations, and a corridor leaves the along-axis translation free. The 2024-2025 field study by Tuna et al. found that plain point-to-plane and Cauchy-kernel ICP "produced broken maps in every experiment". On the Ulmberg tunnel, equality-constrained updates along degenerate directions gave 1.10 m ATE against 2.90 m for plain point-to-plane, at about 19 ms per registration. Every method depended on a good motion prior ([Tuna et al., arXiv 2408.11809](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11809v1)). X-ICP's thresholds are LiDAR correspondence counts and will not transfer to a downsampled depth frame ([X-ICP, T-RO 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.16335)). A noise-derived probabilistic test ([Hatleskog & Alexis, RA-L](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10784)) or the frame-invariant generalized eigenproblem ([arXiv 2608.15532, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15532)) is a better start for stereo. Two integration traps matter here. nvblox is a GPU mapper, up to 177× faster TSDF integration than CPU baselines, but it consumes an external pose and does not localize ([nvblox, ICRA 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00626)). And if the ICP prior is the EKF2 output and the ICP result goes back into EKF2's single EV slot, the system double-counts the same information. Seed ICP from raw VIO and fuse the result through the same correction layer instead. Integrating depth with a pose that later jumps produces doubled walls that corrupt the next registration, so integrate with the smoothed corrected pose. BundleFusion avoids this by re-estimating the model in real time as global poses change ([BundleFusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01093)).

| 4 | Loop closure via GEN_3 `map→odom` (or cuVSLAM), with magnitude gating; survey-then-localize with `.area` | Global error on revisits; repeat missions | Yes | Moderate | ORB-SLAM3, cuVSLAM, Kimera |
| 5 | MAVROS `reset_counter` patch or uXRCE-DDS `VehicleOdometry`, plus offboard `delta_xy` handling | Unavoidable relocalization jumps | n/a | Small patch plus controller work | MAVLink spec, PX4 #19830 |
| 6 | Degeneracy-aware depth ICP to a frozen survey map | Position along visible-wall normals | Yes, or prior map | High | Tuna et al. 2024 |

Learned SLAM systems (DROID-SLAM, DPV-SLAM, MAC-VO) are left out on purpose. They lead vision-only benchmarks, but they use no IMU and need desktop GPUs: DROID-SLAM ran in real time only on two RTX 3090s ([DROID-SLAM, NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/89fcd07f20b6785b92134bd6c1d0fa42-Paper.pdf)). That makes them a poor fit for an onboard Orin flying 15-minute missions.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The ~0.2 m/m cross-track error is not a missing back end. It is an unobserved degree of freedom: with the magnetometer off, nothing on the vehicle measures absolute yaw. Box rooms and warehouses provide a free, absolute yaw reference in every frame that contains a wall. Exploration flights provide few revisits, so a compass that needs one wall will bound error on far more of the flight than loop closure, which needs a return to a known place. Loop closure, prior maps and depth ICP become valuable afterwards, but their benefit depends on the plumbing. On PX4 v1.17 with stock MAVROS, any correction that arrives as a step becomes either a velocity transient or a teleport. The correction layer that integrates continuous odometry and ramps corrections is the one piece of new code every later option depends on.

Three unknowns decide how far this plan goes, and the Isaac Sim bench can answer all three before any hardware flight. How accurate is depth-normal yaw from ZED Mini stereo, rather than structured light? Does GEN_3 close a loop on an in-place 360° spin? How large are GEN_3 `LOOP_CLOSED` corrections in the Warehouse? If the compass comes in near Straub's under-3° real-sensor figure, heading error stops compounding, and the remaining drift becomes VIO translation error along unobserved aisle axes. That residual is small, and loop closure is well suited to clean it up.
